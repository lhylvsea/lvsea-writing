# English AI-smell notes

English drafts use the same fact, intent, reader and voice rules as Chinese drafts. The following map is a review aid, not a list of words that must always be banned.

## High-confidence residue

Remove chat scaffolding, fake citations, placeholder text, hidden Unicode, unexplained citation markup, URL tracking parameters copied into prose, and assistant phrases such as “Here is the revised version” when they are not part of the source.

## Structural patterns

Review clusters rather than isolated words:

- generic significance, “in today's world”, broad trend openings, infomercial hooks and tidy positive endings;
- consultant vocabulary such as leverage, empower, unlock, foster, robust, seamless, game-changing, landscape, delve and crucial when a concrete verb would be clearer;
- repeated “not X but Y”, false ranges, rule-of-three lists, repeated headings, noun-phrase cycling and paragraph reshuffling that hides weak progression;
- passive or subjectless sentences, false agency such as “the data tells us” or “the market rewards”, and over-attributed claims without a named source;
- question titles, fragmented headers, bold-heavy exposition, citation syntax, style shifts between paragraphs and reasoning-chain residue such as “Let me think” or “Breaking this down”.

## Guards

- Keep product names, technical terms, links, code, quotations, citations and house style when they are real requirements.
- Do not ban every em dash, passive sentence, adverb or short sentence. Technical, legal, academic and formal writing may need them.
- Do not make English deliberately broken. Human texture is opt-in and must not change numbers, names, code, links, claims or meaning.
- A statistical score is only a signal. Short samples, quotations, lists, code and specialist prose are especially unreliable.
